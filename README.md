# FiniteChainSyntomicCohomology

Tables of the p-adic K-groups K_r(R; Z_p), 1 ≤ r ≤ 100, of finite chain rings R = O_K/ϖⁿ.
They were computed with [Syntomic.jl](https://github.com/wgabrielong/Syntomic.jl), an
implementation of the algorithm of [AKN, §7.1, pp. 53–57] in

> [AKN] B. Antieau, A. Krause and T. Nikolaus, *On the K-theory of Z/p^n*,
> [arXiv:2405.04329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04329).

There are two families of rings:

- **[Z/pⁿ](#zpn-the-rings-of-akn-appendix-a)**: the 19 rings tabulated in [AKN, Appendix A].
  [AKN] print 799 of their 1,900 groups, and Syntomic.jl gives the same result for every one of
  them. [AKN] mark 494 more with x (not computed within 48 hours). The remaining 607 lie beyond
  the last degree of their tables. So **1,101 groups are new**.
- **[Quadratic extensions of Q₂](#quadratic-extensions-of-q_2)**: O_K/ϖⁿ for the seven quadratic
  extensions K of Q₂ and 2 ≤ n ≤ 5. [AKN] tabulate none of these 28 rings.
  - For n = 2 and K ramified the ring is F₂[z]/z². Hesselholt and Madsen computed its K-groups,
    and the tables agree with their formula.
  - The other **2,200 groups are new** relative to [AKN].
  - O_K/ϖ⁵ for Q₂(√−1) and for Q₂(√3) are not isomorphic, but they have the same K_1–K_100.
    [Coincidences](#coincidences) gives these rings up to isomorphism.

New groups are in **bold** in the tables.

## Quadratic extensions of Q_2

The seven quadratic extensions K of Q₂ are taken from the
[LMFDB](https://www.lmfdb.org/padicField/?p=2&n=2). Each comes with the LMFDB's defining
polynomial:

- For the six ramified fields the polynomial is Eisenstein. It serves as E, so that
  O_K/ϖⁿ = Z₂[z]/(E(z), zⁿ) with ϖ a root of E.
- For the unramified field Q₂(√−3) it presents W(F₄) = Z₂[x]/(x² + x + 1). Then ϖ = 2 and
  O_K/2ⁿ = W_n(F₄).

The ring O_K/ϖⁿ = O_K/𝔪ⁿ does not depend on the choice of ϖ. There is one page per ring:

### Coincidences

<!-- coincidences:begin (generated by scripts/build.jl) -->
For the six ramified fields K, the ring O_K/ϖⁿ consists of the 2ⁿ elements a + bϖ with a mod 2^⌈n/2⌉ and b mod 2^⌊n/2⌋. Two of these rings are isomorphic exactly when one of them contains a uniformizer that is a root of the other's defining polynomial. `quadratic_isomorphic` in [`scripts/build.jl`](scripts/build.jl) searches for such a root among all uniformizers, for every pair. Isomorphic rings have the same K-groups, and the tables agree:

| n | the rings O_K/ϖⁿ up to isomorphism, by K | their K-groups |
|--:|:--|:--|
| 2 | one ring, F₂[z]/z², for all six fields | — |
| 3 | one ring, Z/4[z]/(z² − 2, 2z), for all six fields | — |
| 4 | 2 rings: {Q₂(√−1), Q₂(√3)} · {Q₂(√−2), Q₂(√6), Q₂(√2), Q₂(√−6)} | first differ in K_7 |
| 5 | 3 rings: {Q₂(√−1)} · {Q₂(√3)} · {Q₂(√−2), Q₂(√6), Q₂(√2), Q₂(√−6)} | **the same K_1–K_100** for {Q₂(√−1)} and {Q₂(√3)}, although the rings are not isomorphic; any other two first differ in K_7 |

The unramified field Q₂(√−3) gives W_n(F₄), whose residue field is F₄, so it is none of these rings.

## Reading the tables

- An entry `a₁,a₂,…,a_k` (with a₁ ≤ a₂ ≤ … ≤ a_k) means
  K_r(R; Z_p) ≅ Z/p^a₁ ⊕ Z/p^a₂ ⊕ … ⊕ Z/p^a_k. This is the notation of
  [AKN, Appendix A, p. 61]. For example, the entry `1,1,6` of K_15(Z/3²; Z₃) means
  Z/3 ⊕ Z/3 ⊕ Z/3⁶.
- `0` is the zero group, which [AKN] leave blank. The column *order* gives
  |K_r| = p^(a₁ + … + a_k).
- K_{2i−1}(R; Z_p) ≅ H¹(Z_p(i)(R)) for i ≥ 1, and K_{2i−2}(R; Z_p) ≅ H²(Z_p(i)(R)) for i ≥ 2
  [AKN, §1.1, p. 3; Cor. 2.16, p. 9]. So K_1 to K_100 come from the weights 1 ≤ i ≤ 51.
  K_0(R; Z_p) ≅ Z_p is not listed.
- **Integral K-groups.** If F_q is the residue field of R, the prime-to-p part of K_r(R) is
  that of K_r(F_q) [AKN, §1.1, p. 2]. So K_{2i−1}(R) ≅ K_{2i−1}(R; Z_p) ⊕ Z/(q^i − 1), and
  K_r(R) ≅ K_r(R; Z_p) for even r ≥ 2. Here q = p, except q = 4 for the rings of Q₂(√−3).
- **New** means new relative to the tables of [AKN]. Some groups in low degrees were known
  before. K_1(R; Z_p) is the group of units 1 + 𝔪. For W(F_q)/pⁿ, Angeltveit computed K_r for
  r ≤ 2p − 2 [AKN, §1.2, p. 5].

**`k_theory.json`** has the fields `format` (`"finite-chain-k-theory-v2"`), `akn`,
`conventions` and `rings`. Each ring has these fields:

- `id`, `ring`, and `family`, which is `"akn_appendix_A"` or `"quadratic_Q2"`;
- `field`, `lmfdb_label` and `defining_polynomial`: the field K, with its label and polynomial
  in the LMFDB;
- `p`, `f`, `e`, `n`, `htilde`, `E`: the ring W(F_q)[z]/(E(z), zⁿ), where W(F_q) = Z_p[x]/(h̃).
  `htilde` lists the coefficients of h̃ from low to high degree, and `E[k+1]` the coordinates of
  the coefficient of z^k in the basis 1, x, …, x^(f−1). This is the input of Syntomic.jl's
  `finite_chain_ring(p, htilde, E, n)`;
- `akn`: the section, page, label and last degree of the ring's table in [AKN], or `null`;
- `computation`: the contents of `data/runs`;
- `groups`: one entry per degree, for example

```json
{"r": 25, "exponents": [1,1,1,2,2,3,4,12], "status": "x", "new": true}
```

`exponents` is the sorted list of the a_j, with `[]` for the zero group. `status` is one of the
following:

- `"printed"`, `"x"` or `"beyond"` for the rings of [AKN, Appendix A];
- `"hesselholt_madsen"` for the rings F₂[z]/z²;
- `"not_in_akn"` otherwise.

`new` is true for `"x"`, `"beyond"` and `"not_in_akn"`.

**`k_theory.csv`** has the columns `id,ring,field,p,f,e,n,r,exponents,order_exponent,status,new`.
In `exponents` the a_j are separated by `;`, and the field is empty for the zero group.
`order_exponent` is a₁ + … + a_k.

## How the groups were computed and checked

- **Computation.** For each ring, Syntomic.jl computes the syntomic complexes Z_p(i)(R) of
  [AKN, Thm. 1.1, p. 3] for 1 ≤ i ≤ 51.
  - It uses the algorithm of [AKN, §7.1, pp. 53–57], and takes the cohomology from the Smith
    normal forms of the two differentials.
  - Each ring is one sweep over its weights, `syntomic_sweep(R, [n], 1:51)`. The ring
    `R = finite_chain_ring(p, htilde, E, n)` is built from [`scripts/rings.jl`](scripts/rings.jl).
  - Each sweep runs in a fresh Julia process ([`scripts/compute.jl`](scripts/compute.jl)).
- **Certification.** The cohomology is correct once three conditions hold
  [AKN, Lemma 7.1, p. 57; Remark 7.3, p. 58]:
  - both differentials are known modulo p^M;
  - both have full rank modulo p^M;
  - neither has an elementary divisor of valuation M or more.

  Syntomic.jl checks this for every weight, and raises an error rather than return an answer it
  cannot certify. The working precision of each weight is recorded in `data/cohomology`.
- **Checks.** [`scripts/build.jl`](scripts/build.jl) writes the tables. It stops with an error
  unless all of these hold:
  - the cohomology files are those of the rings of `scripts/rings.jl`;
  - Angeltveit's quotient |H¹(Z_p(i))| / |H²(Z_p(i))| = q^(i(n−1)) [AKN, Prop. 2.18, p. 9]
    holds for every weight of every ring;
  - every group printed in [AKN, Appendix A] equals the computed one (799 groups);
  - for the rings F₂[z]/z², every group equals the one given by Hesselholt and Madsen's formula
    (cited in [AKN, §1.1, p. 2]);
  - isomorphic rings have the same groups (see [Coincidences](#coincidences)).

  The workflow [`check`](.github/workflows/check.yml) reruns `build.jl --check` on every push.
  It fails if any check fails, or if a table no longer matches the data.

## Reproducing

```sh
# the computation of one ring; needs read access to Syntomic.jl, which the git command line
# (JULIA_PKG_USE_CLI_GIT=true) has with the usual GitHub credentials
JULIA_PKG_USE_CLI_GIT=true julia --project=scripts/compute -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()'
julia --project=scripts/compute -t 8 scripts/compute.jl zpn_7_7        # or Q2_sqrt-1_n5, …

# the tables, the JSON and CSV files, and the tables of rings above
julia --project=scripts -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()'
julia --project=scripts scripts/build.jl
```

`scripts/compute/Project.toml` pins the Syntomic.jl commit that computed the data. The time and
memory each ring needs are in the tables above.
