# FiniteChainSyntomicCohomology

Tables of the p-adic K-groups K_r(R; Z_p), 1 ≤ r ≤ 100, of finite chain rings R = O_K/ϖⁿ.
They were computed with [Syntomic.jl](https://github.com/wgabrielong/Syntomic.jl), an
implementation of the algorithm of [AKN, §7.1, pp. 53–57] in

> [AKN] B. Antieau, A. Krause and T. Nikolaus, *On the K-theory of Z/p^n*,
> [arXiv:2405.04329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04329).

There are three families of rings, with one page of K-groups per ring:

- **[Z/pⁿ](#zpn-the-rings-of-akn-appendix-a)**: the 19 rings tabulated in [AKN, Appendix A].
  [AKN] print 799 of their 1,900 groups, and Syntomic.jl gives the same result for every one of
  them. [AKN] mark 494 more with x (not computed within 48 hours). The remaining 607 lie beyond
  the last degree of their tables. So **1,101 groups are new**.
- **[Quadratic extensions of Q_p](#quadratic-extensions-of-q_p)**: O_K/ϖⁿ for 2 ≤ n ≤ 5 and the
  16 quadratic extensions K of Q₂, Q₃, Q₅ and Q₇, 64 rings. **5,200 groups are new**. The other
  1,200 are those of the rings O_K/ϖ² = F_p[z]/z², which Hesselholt and Madsen computed.
- **[Cubic extensions of Q_p](#cubic-extensions-of-q_p)**: O_K/ϖⁿ for 2 ≤ n ≤ 5 and the 18
  cubic extensions K of Q₂, Q₃, Q₅ and Q₇, 72 rings. **4,400 groups are new**. The other
  2,800 are those of the rings O_K/ϖ² = F_p[z]/z² and O_K/ϖ³ = F_p[z]/z³, which Hesselholt and
  Madsen computed.

[AKN] tabulate none of the rings of the quadratic and cubic fields. New groups are in **bold**
in the tables.

Different fields can give the same ring O_K/ϖⁿ, and different rings can have the same
K-groups. [Coincidences](docs/coincidences.md) lists them all. Among them:

- O_K/ϖ⁵ for Q₂(√−1) and for Q₂(√3) are not isomorphic, but they have the same K_1–K_100.
- F₂[z]/z³ = O_K/ϖ³ for Q₂(∛2) and Z/4[z]/(z² − 2, 2z) = O_K/ϖ³ for every ramified quadratic
  extension of Q₂ are not isomorphic, but they have the same K_1–K_100.
- The nine ramified cubic extensions of Q₃ give a single ring O_K/ϖ⁴, and three rings O_K/ϖ⁵.
  Two of these, for Q₃(∛2) and Q₃[x]/(x³ + 6x + 3), have the same K_1–K_100.
- O_K/ϖ⁴ and O_K/ϖ⁵ for Q₇(∛14) and for Q₇(∛21) are not isomorphic, but they have the same
  K_1–K_100. Those of Q₇(∛7) first differ from them in K_3.
- For odd p, the two ramified quadratic fields give different rings for every n ≥ 3, and their
  K-groups differ in low degree: in K_1 or K_2 for p = 3, in K_3 for p = 5 and in K_5 for
  p = 7.

More detail:

- [The quadratic and cubic fields](docs/fields.md): where they come from, how their rings are
  presented, and their names.
- [Coincidences](docs/coincidences.md): the rings up to isomorphism, and the different rings
  with the same K-groups.
- [How the groups were computed and checked](docs/methods.md).
- [The data files](docs/data-format.md): the fields of `k_theory.json` and `k_theory.csv`.

## Reading the tables

- An entry `a₁,a₂,…,a_k` (with a₁ ≤ a₂ ≤ … ≤ a_k) means
  K_r(R; Z_p) ≅ Z/p^a₁ ⊕ Z/p^a₂ ⊕ … ⊕ Z/p^a_k. This is the notation of
  [AKN, Appendix A, p. 61]. For example, the entry `1,1,6` of K_15(Z/3²; Z₃) means
  Z/3 ⊕ Z/3 ⊕ Z/3⁶.
- `0` is the zero group, which [AKN] leave blank. The column *order* gives
  |K_r| = p^(a₁ + … + a_k).
- K_{2i−1}(R; Z_p) ≅ H¹(Z_p(i)(R)) for i ≥ 1, and K_{2i−2}(R; Z_p) ≅ H²(Z_p(i)(R)) for i ≥ 2
  [AKN, §1.1, p. 3; Cor. 2.16, p. 9]. So K_1 to K_100 come from the weights 1 ≤ i ≤ 51.
  K_0(R; Z_p) ≅ Z_p is not listed.
- **Integral K-groups.** If F_q is the residue field of R, the prime-to-p part of K_r(R) is
  that of K_r(F_q) [AKN, §1.1, p. 2]. So K_{2i−1}(R) ≅ K_{2i−1}(R; Z_p) ⊕ Z/(q^i − 1), and
  K_r(R) ≅ K_r(R; Z_p) for even r ≥ 2. Here q = p, except q = p² and q = p³ for the rings of the
  unramified quadratic and cubic fields.
- **New** means new relative to the tables of [AKN]. Some groups in low degrees were known
  before. K_1(R; Z_p) is the group of units 1 + 𝔪. For W(F_q)/pⁿ, Angeltveit computed K_r for
  r ≤ 2p − 2 [AKN, §1.2, p. 5].

## Reproducing

```sh
# the computation of one ring; needs read access to Syntomic.jl, which the git command line
# (JULIA_PKG_USE_CLI_GIT=true) has with the usual GitHub credentials
JULIA_PKG_USE_CLI_GIT=true julia --project=scripts/compute -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()'
julia --project=scripts/compute -t 8 scripts/compute.jl zpn_7_7        # or Q2_sqrt-1_n5, Q3_cbrt2_n5, …

# the tables, the JSON and CSV files, and the generated parts of README.md and docs/
julia --project=scripts -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()'
julia --project=scripts scripts/build.jl
```

`scripts/compute/Project.toml` pins the Syntomic.jl commit that computed the data. The time and
memory each ring needs are in the tables above.
