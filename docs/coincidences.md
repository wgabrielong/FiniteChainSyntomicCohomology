# Coincidences

[Back to the tables](../README.md)

Different fields K can give the same ring O_K/ϖⁿ, and different rings can have the same
K-groups. This page lists both. Isomorphic rings have the same K-groups, and
[`scripts/build.jl`](../scripts/build.jl) checks that their tables agree.

Two rings O_K/ϖⁿ with the same p and n are isomorphic only if they have the same residue field
F_q and, unless both are F_q[z]/zⁿ (n ≤ e), the same ramification index e: if n > e, then p
lies in 𝔪^e but not in 𝔪^{e+1}. So the unramified fields, whose rings are the W_n(F_q), give no
coincidences, and the ramified fields of one degree are compared below.

## Among the quadratic rings

<!-- quadratic-coincidences:begin (generated by scripts/build.jl) -->
For a ramified quadratic extension K of Q_p, the ring O_K/ϖⁿ consists of the pⁿ elements a + bϖ with a mod p^⌈n/2⌉ and b mod p^⌊n/2⌋. Two such rings are isomorphic exactly when one of them contains a uniformizer that is a root of the other's defining polynomial. `ramified_isomorphic` in [`scripts/build.jl`](../scripts/build.jl) searches for such a root among all uniformizers, for every pair. Isomorphic rings have the same K-groups, and the tables agree:

| p | n | the rings O_K/ϖⁿ up to isomorphism, by K | their K-groups |
|--:|--:|:--|:--|
| 2 | 2 | one ring, F₂[z]/z², for all 6 fields | — |
| 2 | 3 | one ring, Z/4[z]/(z² − 2, 2z), for all 6 fields | — |
| 2 | 4 | 2 rings: {Q₂(√−1), Q₂(√3)} · {Q₂(√−2), Q₂(√6), Q₂(√2), Q₂(√−6)} | first differ in K_7 |
| 2 | 5 | 3 rings: {Q₂(√−1)} · {Q₂(√3)} · {Q₂(√−2), Q₂(√6), Q₂(√2), Q₂(√−6)} | **the same K_1–K_100** for {Q₂(√−1)} and {Q₂(√3)}, although the rings are not isomorphic; any other two first differ in K_7 |
| 3 | 2 | one ring, F₃[z]/z², for both fields | — |
| 3 | 3 | 2 rings: {Q₃(√−3)} · {Q₃(√3)} | first differ in K_2 |
| 3 | 4 | 2 rings: {Q₃(√−3)} · {Q₃(√3)} | first differ in K_1 |
| 3 | 5 | 2 rings: {Q₃(√−3)} · {Q₃(√3)} | first differ in K_1 |
| 5 | 2 | one ring, F₅[z]/z², for both fields | — |
| 5 | 3 | 2 rings: {Q₅(√5)} · {Q₅(√10)} | first differ in K_3 |
| 5 | 4 | 2 rings: {Q₅(√5)} · {Q₅(√10)} | first differ in K_3 |
| 5 | 5 | 2 rings: {Q₅(√5)} · {Q₅(√10)} | first differ in K_3 |
| 7 | 2 | one ring, F₇[z]/z², for both fields | — |
| 7 | 3 | 2 rings: {Q₇(√−7)} · {Q₇(√7)} | first differ in K_5 |
| 7 | 4 | 2 rings: {Q₇(√−7)} · {Q₇(√7)} | first differ in K_5 |

## Among the cubic rings

<!-- cubic-coincidences:begin (generated by scripts/build.jl) -->
For a ramified cubic extension K of Q_p, the ring O_K/ϖⁿ consists of the pⁿ elements a + bϖ + cϖ² with a mod p^⌈n/3⌉, b mod p^⌈(n−1)/3⌉ and c mod p^⌈(n−2)/3⌉. Two such rings are isomorphic exactly when one of them contains a uniformizer that is a root of the other's defining polynomial. `ramified_isomorphic` in [`scripts/build.jl`](../scripts/build.jl) searches for such a root among all uniformizers, for every pair. Isomorphic rings have the same K-groups, and the tables agree:

| p | n | the rings O_K/ϖⁿ up to isomorphism, by K | their K-groups |
|--:|--:|:--|:--|
| 3 | 2 | one ring, F₃[z]/z², for all 9 fields | — |
| 3 | 3 | one ring, F₃[z]/z³, for all 9 fields | — |
| 3 | 4 | one ring for all 9 fields | — |
| 3 | 5 | 3 rings: {Q₃(∛2)} · {Q₃[x]/(x³ + 6x + 3)} · {Q₃[x]/(x³ + 3x² + 3), Q₃(ζ₉)⁺, Q₃[x]/(x³ + 6x² + 12), Q₃[x]/(x³ + 6x² + 21), Q₃(∛3), Q₃(∛12), Q₃(∛6)} | **the same K_1–K_100** for {Q₃(∛2)} and {Q₃[x]/(x³ + 6x + 3)}, although the rings are not isomorphic; any other two first differ in K_11 |
| 7 | 2 | one ring, F₇[z]/z², for all 3 fields | — |
| 7 | 3 | one ring, F₇[z]/z³, for all 3 fields | — |
| 7 | 4 | 3 rings: {Q₇(∛7)} · {Q₇(∛14)} · {Q₇(∛21)} | **the same K_1–K_100** for {Q₇(∛14)} and {Q₇(∛21)}, although the rings are not isomorphic; any other two first differ in K_3 |
| 7 | 5 | 3 rings: {Q₇(∛7)} · {Q₇(∛14)} · {Q₇(∛21)} | **the same K_1–K_100** for {Q₇(∛14)} and {Q₇(∛21)}, although the rings are not isomorphic; any other two first differ in K_3 |

Q₂ and Q₅ each have one ramified cubic field, so they are not in the table.
